    v6.0 Research Engine — Mathematical Core
    Objective: estimate a time-varying predictive distribution rather than one deterministic target. Daily log returns use Bayesian-shrunk drift and AR(1), conditional variance uses GJR-GARCH(1,1), shocks use Student-t innovations, and a 3-state duration-aware latent regime model lets the drift/volatility environment switch through time, with exit probabilities that depend on how long a regime has persisted. Tens of thousands of sequential Monte Carlo particles propagate those hidden regimes day by day.
    Mean / drift
    μ_post = (N μ_hist + k μ_prior)/(N + k + 60). The extra zero-drift shrinkage is deliberate: expected short-horizon equity returns are extremely noisy to estimate.
    Conditional variance
    hₜ = ω + αε²ₜ₋₁ + γε²ₜ₋₁I(εₜ₋₁<0) + βhₜ₋₁. The γ term allows negative shocks to affect future volatility differently.
    Heavy tails
    εₜ = √hₜ zₜ with standardized Student-t zₜ. Degrees of freedom ν are calibrated by likelihood, allowing fatter tails than Gaussian GBM.
    Tail risk
    VaR 95%, Expected Shortfall 95%, probability of loss and P5–P95 dispersion are computed directly from the simulated terminal-price distribution.
    Research lineage: Engle (1982) ARCH · Bollerslev (1986, 1987) GARCH / Student-t · Glosten, Jagannathan & Runkle (1993) asymmetric volatility · Hansen (1994) conditional Student-t density · J.P. Morgan RiskMetrics (1996) EWMA diagnostics · Welch & Goyal (2008; updated 2024) out-of-sample caution on equity-return predictability.
    Limitation: daily historical prices alone do not make individual-stock returns reliably predictable. v6 improves volatility/tail calibration; it does not create a guaranteed directional edge. Proper walk-forward backtesting and richer data remain necessary.
